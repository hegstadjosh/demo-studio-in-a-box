# Project file

After a first look at the material, and before any other production artifact, create `project.md` and, beside it, `project.json`. Decisions held only in conversation are lost when context is compacted or a helper starts fresh, so gate scripts read `project.json`, helper briefs quote `project.md`, and both are updated with every decision. The standard is a producer's notes: an editor joining mid-project could continue from them alone. Write the sections a helper or the next artifact will read; the short form, for a job one session can finish, holds goal, format, truth list and directives.

`project.json` holds what scripts read, since a script cannot reliably parse prose: budget fields (section 6), whether the person is at the machine (section 3), directives with id, status and draft id, held renders, current draft id, last shown draft id.

## Sections

1. **Goal.** One sentence naming the audience and what it should take away or do. **Hypothesis:** propose it in standard communication terms (awareness, interest, understanding, desire to use a product), which name an outcome for the whole film without prescribing a scene. In the promotional film this package was distilled from (the source production), repeated rewording ended in those terms; other genres are untested. It fails when the sentence names a scene or loses its outcome words. For a goal that is not promotional, write the outcome in the person's words.
2. **Forest line.** Goal, premise and current stage in one line, which the status block prints.
3. **Person.** Availability as they described it, channel, review device, desired involvement, whether they will direct cuts (which bears on building the edit room), and whether they are at the machine or review through the channel; `tools/show` reads this to open a file in front of them or send it.
4. **Material and assets.** Read the person's notes first, including what earlier films did well. Keep an asset register, one line per asset, existing or coming: kind, path, what it depicts. Date and attribute source fragments; label a synthesis as yours. A single note, however emphatic, is one dated fragment of the person's intent, not a maintained vision; weigh it against their later messages. **Hypothesis:** search the register before making an asset of a kind, and paste it into every helper brief that creates assets. In the source production, duplicate characters, clip sets and scripts were made mid-project by agents that never saw the earlier ones, which a first-look inspection cannot reach. Whether a register stops this is untested; it fails when an asset is made without its line, so add the line when writing the file.
5. **Truth list.** Settle before building whether screens are recordings or depictions, and whether sample content, numbers and real people may appear. **Must (truthfulness):** mark each element recorded, cut or reordered, authored (scores, labels, comments), designed depiction, proposed experience (not yet in the product), or generated; mark each product claim verified, with its source, or assumed. **Must (permission):** record consent and rights for every real person and third-party clip before it appears in a deliverable (G5). Material the person supplied for this film is presumed cleared for this film: record it as "supplied by the person, presumed cleared, not confirmed" and list it in the truth note.
6. **Budget.** This section owns the budget rule. **Must (permission):** for third-party paid services (transcription, voice, music, image or video generation), record the authorized amount, the services the person named, whether helper token cost counts, and whether the budget pre-approves each call within it or only caps the total; with nothing authorized, it is zero (G2). Your own model calls, helpers included, on the harness or account the person launched you on are the cost of doing the job: `tools/spend` logs them and G2 does not gate them, unless `local.md` or the person says helper token cost counts. A fresh model call given only the goal, the assembly script and the export counts as the independent reader for G11.
7. **Format.** Platform, length band, aspect, captions; mark choices the person did not make as agent choices.
8. **Plan and gates installed**: the project's level, the next artifacts the plan depends on, and the gates (`SKILL.md`, `seeds/gates.md`).
9. **Decision ledger.** Each entry: content; scope (layer, section, draft id); source (the person's words closely paraphrased, or "agent choice, open to veto"); status (proposed, accepted, implemented, reviewed, superseded), which keeps suggestions, decisions, implementation and reviewed output distinct. Refer to drafts by id and keep old ones, since the person compares versions; list separately the favourites the person wants kept. **Must (truthfulness):** never change a source from agent choice to the person's decision without their word; correct wrong entries in place. **Must (explicit requirement):** do not reinstate a rejected scene from an older plan or silently remove a favourite to meet a duration the person never required; telling them afterwards is not asking. **Hypothesis:** a correction to one dimension (format, tone, one caption layer) changes only that dimension; record that scope and carry the rest forward. In the source production one format correction made the agent discard a settled plan. If the person meant more, the next check-in packet shows your reading and they correct it.
10. **Directive ledger.** Each instruction with its id, status and the draft id carrying it, including held renders and their release (G4). Every entry in the verbatim inbox, `messages.md`, links to a directive id or is marked "no directive" (G9). Make the link before acting on the message, so each instruction is in this ledger before work on it starts. **Must (explicit requirement):** the latest explicit instruction overrides any earlier entry and any plan. **Hypothesis:** paste the entries in force into every helper brief; in the source production a work plan went stale under later instructions, and a helper lost decisions it never received. During rapid dictation one entry may hold a whole order. It fails when several agents write the files from stale reads; let one agent write them, the others report to that agent, and reread before each write.
11. **Methods that worked**, stated so they carry over to new content.
12. **Rejected directions**, as explicit as accepted ones.

## Ledger diff and check

At each showing, `tools/show` copies `project.json` to `packets/<draft-id>.project.json` and puts its comparison with the last shown draft's snapshot into the check-in packet (G9), so a misread instruction surfaces while it is cheap to fix. To check both files, give them alone to a fresh-context reader and ask for the goal, open directives and held renders; a wrong answer marks the section to fix.

## Intake

**Hypothesis.** If the person is reachable, send, after looking at the material, one message of numbered questions, each answerable in a word, asking only what you cannot find out and what would change your next artifact: audience and effect; platform and format; material existing or coming; budget, paid services and accounts; consent and clip rights; how the person reviews and how involved they want to be; whether they will direct cuts; where the product's real facts live. In the source production each arrived later as a separate correction from a director who answered quickly; which matter elsewhere is unknown. Questions sent before looking come out generic. **Must (explicit requirement):** when the person has said not to involve them, ask nothing; choose, and record each choice as an agent choice.
